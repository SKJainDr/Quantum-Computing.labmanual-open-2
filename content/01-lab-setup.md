<h1 id="introduction-to-quantum-computing-laboratory-ii">Introduction to Quantum Computing Laboratory II</h1><h2 id="11-from-foundations-to-the-frontier">1.1  From Foundations to the Frontier</h2><p>Quantum Computing Laboratory I provided the conceptual and practical foundation: superposition, entanglement, density matrices, noise models, and introductory algorithms. Laboratory II begins where those foundations end — at the cutting edge of what quantum processors can achieve today and the algorithmic and error-correction techniques that will define quantum computing's near-term and long-term impact.</p><p>The central challenge of contemporary quantum computing is the NISQ problem: Noisy Intermediate-Scale Quantum devices have 50–1000 qubits but high gate error rates (~0.1–1% per two-qubit gate) and short coherence times. This lab series directly addresses this challenge through three complementary approaches:</p><ul><li><p>Characterisation: understand and quantify the noise (Experiments 16–18)</p></li><li><p>Algorithm Design: use variational, hybrid, and near-term-friendly algorithms (Experiments 19–21, 24–25, 26, 29)</p></li><li><p>Error Correction and Mitigation: suppress or correct errors at the circuit or post-processing level (Experiments 18, 22–23, 28, 29)</p></li></ul><h2 id="12-experiment-map">1.2  Experiment Map</h2><table>

Authenticate: QiskitRuntimeService(channel='ibm_quantum')</p><p>2. Select backend: service.least_busy(operational=True, simulator=False)</p><p>3. Read calibration: backend.properties() — record T1, T2, gate errors</p><p>4. Transpile: generate_preset_pass_manager(backend, optimization_level=3)</p><p>5. Submit: sampler.run([isa_qc], shots=N) — record job.job_id()</p><p>6.
